The West Highland Line is the best train ride in Britain

Lochs, moors and a famous viaduct: five hours from Glasgow to the sea on an ordinary timetabled train.

By Tom Lindqvist, trail and rail writer

27 August 2026 · 2 min read

In this story
  1. Glasgow to Rannoch Moor
  2. Fort William and the viaduct
  3. To the end of the line
  4. Riding the line

You do not need a luxury tour to see the best of the Scottish Highlands. The ordinary train from Glasgow to Mallaig takes a little over five hours, and it might be the most beautiful railway journey in Britain.

I rode it in late August with a flask of tea, a window seat and no plan beyond the end of the line.

Glasgow to Rannoch Moor

The train leaves the city and almost immediately follows the shores of lochs, before climbing into the hills. By the time you reach Rannoch Moor, there are no roads in sight, just bog, water and sky.

Rannoch station is one of the most remote in the country. A few people got off with huge packs and walked straight into the emptiness.

Fort William and the viaduct

After Fort William the line turns west towards the sea and crosses the Glenfinnan Viaduct, the curved stone bridge many people know from films. The train slows down, phones come out and everyone on one side of the carriage leans across the other side.

In summer, a steam train runs on the same line. It is lovely, but it books up months ahead. The ordinary train gives you the same view for much less.

By the time you reach Rannoch Moor, there are no roads in sight, just bog, water and sky.

To the end of the line

The last stretch to Mallaig passes white beaches and views out to the islands. Mallaig itself is a small fishing port, with ferries to Skye and the smaller isles.

I ate fish and chips on the harbour wall, watched the ferry come in and took the train back the next morning. I would do it again tomorrow.

Riding the line

  • Book a seat reservation, especially in summer.
  • Bring food and drink; there is often no trolley service.
  • Consider breaking the journey at Fort William for a night.
  • Combine it with the ferry from Mallaig to Skye.
