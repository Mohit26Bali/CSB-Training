Kyoto belongs to early risers

The famous shrines and bamboo groves are packed by ten. At six, you might have them to yourself.

By Clara Mendes, editor and city walker

17 September 2026 · 2 min read

In this story
  1. Fushimi Inari at sunrise
  2. Arashiyama's bamboo grove
  3. Slow afternoons
  4. Early-morning Kyoto

Kyoto is one of the most visited cities in the world, and its most famous places can feel more like a queue than a temple. But the crowds keep office hours. If you get up early, the city is completely different.

For a week I set my alarm for five, and it changed the whole trip.

Fushimi Inari at sunrise

The shrine at Fushimi Inari never closes, and its paths of orange gates wind all the way up a wooded mountain. By late morning the lower gates are shoulder to shoulder. At six, I met two joggers and a cat.

Most visitors turn back at the first viewpoint. If you keep climbing, the gates thin out, small stone foxes guard the path and the city slowly appears below you.

Arashiyama's bamboo grove

The bamboo grove is short, only a few minutes' walk, which is exactly why it gets so crowded. Early in the morning the light comes through the stems in green stripes, and you can hear the bamboo creaking in the wind.

Afterwards I walked along the river and had breakfast as the shops opened. By the time I came back past the grove at ten, I could not see the path for people.

The crowds keep office hours. If you get up early, the city is completely different.

Slow afternoons

Getting up early has a reward: the afternoon is free when everywhere is busy. I spent mine in small neighbourhood temples, coffee shops and public baths, the places tour buses do not stop.

I also slept a lot. Nobody tells you that a dawn-to-dusk trip needs a nap.

Early-morning Kyoto

  • Fushimi Inari is open around the clock; aim to arrive by 6.30.
  • Take the first train to Arashiyama and reach the grove by 7.
  • Stay near a station so early starts are easy.
  • Pack breakfast the night before, as few cafés open before 8.
