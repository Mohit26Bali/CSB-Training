Falling asleep in Vienna, waking up in Venice

The night train over the Alps is cheaper than a hotel and a flight, and the breakfast comes with a view.

By Sofia Marchetti, rail and budget columnist

2 October 2026 · 2 min read

In this story
  1. Choosing a bed
  2. The night, and the morning
  3. Was it worth it?
  4. Night train checklist

There is something wonderful about going to bed in one country and waking up in another. The night train from Vienna to Venice leaves in the evening and arrives the next morning, crossing the Alps while you sleep.

I booked a couchette, the cheapest bed on the train, and kept a note of every cost so I could compare it honestly with flying.

Choosing a bed

There are three choices. A seat is cheapest but you will not sleep much. A couchette is a shared compartment with four or six simple bunks, bedding included. A sleeper is a private cabin with a washbasin, and sometimes a shower.

I chose a four-berth couchette and shared it with a retired teacher from Graz and two students heading to a wedding. Everyone was asleep by eleven, which is more than I can say for most hostels.

The night, and the morning

The train is slow and steady. I woke once somewhere in the mountains to see a tiny station lit up in the snow, then fell asleep again. The rocking is gentle once you stop noticing it.

Breakfast arrived in a paper bag: bread, jam, butter and a hot drink. By the time I had finished it, the mountains had flattened into farmland, and soon after that we were rolling across the long bridge over the lagoon.

There is something wonderful about going to bed in one country and waking up in another.

Was it worth it?

Once I added up the costs, the train was cheaper than a flight and one night in a central Venice hotel, and I arrived in the middle of the city instead of at an airport. Booked early, a couchette costs about the same as a budget hotel room.

The only real downside is that the trains sell out early in summer. Book as soon as tickets go on sale, usually a few months ahead.

Night train checklist

  • Book as soon as sales open; summer couchettes sell out first.
  • Bring earplugs, an eye mask and a bottle of water.
  • Keep your passport and phone in a small bag that sleeps with you.
  • Couchettes are shared and mixed unless you book a women-only compartment.
