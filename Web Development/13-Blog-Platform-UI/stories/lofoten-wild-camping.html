Wild camping under the midnight sun in Lofoten

Norway's right to roam lets you pitch a tent almost anywhere. Here is how to do it well in Lofoten.

By Tom Lindqvist, trail and rail writer

12 August 2026 · 2 min read

In this story
  1. The right to roam
  2. Hiking at midnight
  3. Leave no trace
  4. Camping in Lofoten

The Lofoten islands rise straight out of the sea in a line of sharp, green peaks, with fishing villages tucked into every bay. In midsummer the sun does not set at all, and you can hike at midnight in golden light.

I spent a week there with a tent, using Norway's right to roam to camp on beaches and ridges.

The right to roam

Norway's outdoor access law lets anyone camp on uncultivated land for free, as long as they are at least 150 metres from homes and cabins and move on after two nights. It is a generous right, and it depends on people using it respectfully.

In the busiest spots, especially some popular beaches, local rules now limit camping. Look for signs, and if a place looks worn out, go somewhere else.

Hiking at midnight

Without darkness, your days lose their shape. I found myself starting hikes at eight in the evening and reaching ridges at midnight, with the sun hanging low over the sea instead of setting.

Bring an eye mask. Sleeping in a tent in full daylight is harder than it sounds.

In midsummer the sun does not set at all, and you can hike at midnight in golden light.

Leave no trace

There are few bins and fewer toilets on the trails. Carry out all your rubbish, use toilets in villages whenever you can and never light fires on rock or in dry grass.

The islands are beautiful because people have looked after them. Leave your camp spot looking as if you were never there.

Camping in Lofoten

  • Pitch at least 150 metres from homes and cabins.
  • Move on after two nights in one place.
  • Bring an eye mask for the midnight sun.
  • Carry out everything you carry in.
