Eating my way through Hanoi, one plastic stool at a time

Pho at six, bun cha at noon and egg coffee at four: how to eat like a local in Hanoi's Old Quarter.

By Dev Raman, food writer

28 September 2026 · 2 min read

In this story
  1. Six in the morning: pho
  2. Noon: bun cha
  3. Four in the afternoon: egg coffee
  4. Street food, safely

Hanoi eats on the pavement. In the Old Quarter, every doorway seems to be a kitchen, and every kitchen does one dish, the same way, every day, often for decades.

I spent five days eating my way around those streets. The rule I followed was simple: find the stall with the most locals and the smallest menu, and sit down.

Six in the morning: pho

Pho is breakfast. By six the best stalls are already busy, with a huge pot of broth that has been simmering all night. You get a bowl of rice noodles and beef, and you add herbs, lime and chilli yourself.

Most good pho stalls close by mid-morning, once the broth runs out. If you turn up at lunch, you have missed it.

Noon: bun cha

At lunchtime the smoke starts. Bun cha is grilled pork patties and pork belly in a sweet, sour broth, served with cold rice noodles and a basket of herbs. You dip the noodles into the broth, a little at a time.

The stalls grill over charcoal right on the pavement, so follow your nose. A full lunch with a drink cost me about the same as a coffee back home.

Find the stall with the most locals and the smallest menu, and sit down.

Four in the afternoon: egg coffee

Egg coffee sounds strange and tastes like dessert. Egg yolks are whisked with sugar and condensed milk until they are thick and creamy, then spooned over strong coffee.

The best places are up narrow staircases or hidden at the back of shops. It is worth getting a little lost to find one.

Street food, safely

  • Choose busy stalls: a fast turnover means fresh food.
  • Cooked-to-order and boiling-hot dishes are the safest choice.
  • Carry small notes; most stalls cannot change large ones.
  • Point and smile. A menu is rare, and a single dish is normal.
