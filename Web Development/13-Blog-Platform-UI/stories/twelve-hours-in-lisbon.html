Twelve hours in Lisbon, on foot and by tram

Seven hills, one old yellow tram and far too many custard tarts: a full day in Lisbon without a taxi.

By Clara Mendes, editor and city walker

6 October 2026 · 2 min read

In this story
  1. Morning: Alfama before the crowds
  2. Midday: Tram 28 and the climb to Graça
  3. Evening: Belém, then fado
  4. Plan your Lisbon day

Lisbon is a city that rewards people who walk, and punishes them a little too. The hills are steep, the pavements are polished stone and every viewpoint is at the top of something. But in twelve hours you can see most of the old city on foot, with a tram or two to save your legs.

I started at seven in the morning, when the streets are still being washed and the cafés are only just pulling up their shutters.

Morning: Alfama before the crowds

Alfama is the oldest part of the city, and it is best before nine. The lanes twist downhill in a way no map can really explain, so I stopped trying and followed the washing lines. Washing hangs between the windows, cats sleep on doorsteps and the only sound is someone sweeping.

Breakfast was a coffee and a pastel de nata standing at a counter, which is how most people here have it. It cost less than two euros, and I am still thinking about it.

Midday: Tram 28 and the climb to Graça

Tram 28 is famous, which means it is busy. The trick is to catch it near the start of its route at Martim Moniz, where you can actually get a seat, and ride it uphill instead of walking. It rattles past churches and shop doorways close enough to touch.

I got off at Graça and walked up to the Senhora do Monte viewpoint, the highest in the city. It is quieter than the well-known terraces lower down, and you can see the whole old town, the castle and the river at once.

Nobody spoke while she sang. Then everyone talked at once, and dinner carried on.

Evening: Belém, then fado

In the late afternoon I took the riverside tram out to Belém for the monastery and the riverside tower. The queue for the famous custard tart shop moves faster than it looks, and yes, it is worth it.

I ended the day back in Alfama, in a small restaurant where the owner's cousin sang fado between courses. Nobody spoke while she sang. Then everyone talked at once, and dinner carried on.

Plan your Lisbon day

  • Wear shoes with grip: the stone pavements are slippery even when they are dry.
  • A day travel card covers trams, buses, the metro and the city's funiculars.
  • Board Tram 28 at Martim Moniz, near the start of the line, to get a seat.
  • Many small fado restaurants do not take bookings, so arrive by eight.
