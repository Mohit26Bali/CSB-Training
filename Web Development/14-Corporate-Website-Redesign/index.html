Website redesign case study

Fernhill College: from 2012 to today

The same college, the same courses and the same facts, presented two ways. The old site was built like many college websites around 2012. The redesign fixes its accessibility, responsiveness and readability, and every improvement below was measured, not guessed.

The results

Accessibility problems were counted with axe-core (the engine behind many accessibility checkers) on every page, testing against WCAG 2.2 level AA. The other figures were measured in the browser on each home page.

  • Accessibility problems found by axe

    410 → 0

    Failing elements across the 4 old pages (10 to 12 failed rules on every page), against 0 on all 5 new pages. The old count moves by about 1 between runs, depending on which slide the slider is showing.

  • Main text size

    11px → 18px

    94% of the old text was smaller than 14px; none of the new text is

  • Body text contrast

    2.85:1 → 15.2:1

    WCAG AA needs at least 4.5:1 for normal text

  • Sideways scrolling on a narrow screen

    464px → 0px

    In a 496px window the old fixed 960px layout overflowed by 464px

  • Images with no alt text

    26 → 0

    On the home page, including 8 images that contained text, like the menu buttons

  • Headings on the home page

    0 → 21

    The old "headings" were bold text, so screen reader users could not jump between sections

What was wrong, and how it was fixed

Each problem is linked to the WCAG 2.2 success criterion it breaks.

Rewriting for readability

The facts stayed the same; the words changed. Compare the first thing each home page says:

“Fernhill College is a leading provider of world-class, learner-centred further education and training solutions, delivering innovative and flexible programmes of study across a diverse range of vocational and academic curriculum areas to over 4,200 learners each year.”

Before: 1 sentence, 37 words, full of jargon

“Learn the skills that get you hired. Practical courses for school leavers, adults and apprentices, taught by people who have worked in the industry.”

After: 2 short sentences that say who it is for and what they get
